# B - Apps like ours: who converts women 55+ from Facebook, and how

Agent B, Nov 13, 2026. Only agent using the Meta Ad Library (loaded one page at a time, US, active ads, sorted by impressions where possible).
Tags: **[verified]** = I saw it myself today. **[reported]** = a third party says so. **[guess]** = my inference.
Prior reports (P3, R3, g1 teardown) already cover Bend, Muscle Booster's in-app paywall, Hinge/Sword/Kaia models and the competitor's store prices. This report only adds what is new.

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **The apps that win with women 55+ on Facebook mostly do NOT send them to the App Store.** Every Yoga-Go and Muscle Booster ad with a visible link goes to a **web quiz** (plan.yoga-go.io, plan.muscle-booster.io). BetterMe's Chair Yoga ads go to betterme-chair-yoga.com, a web quiz. The person pays on the web, then downloads the app. [verified, Ad Library Nov 13]
- **The web quiz is age-first and built for 50-70+.** BetterMe Chair Yoga: screen 1 "Chair Yoga Workout Plan - CHOOSE YOUR AGE: 40-49 / 50-59 / 60-69 / 70+". Yoga-Go: "35-44 / 45-54 / 55-64 / 65+". WalkFit (reported): "25-49 / 50-59 / 60-69 / 70+". Our own competitor's new quiz (heelpaintrainer.com) also asks age first. [verified / reported]
- **No free trial at all on the web.** BetterMe Chair Yoga ends at a paywall with a **paid** first period: 1 week $6.93, 4 weeks $15.19 (preselected, "MOST POPULAR"), 12 weeks $36.99, all "61% OFF", 10-minute countdown, then renews at $38.95 every 4 weeks. WalkFit (reported) uses the same shape in euros. [verified BetterMe]
- **The volume is huge and fast.** In September 2026 BetterMe launched ~169,400 web2app ads, WalkFit ~115,500, Yoga-Go ~99,300, Lasta ~66,500 (SpyTrend, reported). Yoga-Go's US page has ~540 active ads today and its top ads by impressions all started Nov 9-12, so they rotate creative every few days. [verified count / reported volume]
- **Their creatives are mostly things Walkito must not copy:** AI drama videos (a 6-minute AI story of a woman shamed on a plane, "Day 28" body reveal), before/after bodies on the paywall, "visible results in 4 weeks", weight-loss promises, fake-feeling "promo code applied" + countdown. [verified]
- **Two clean patterns that Walkito CAN copy:** (a) a real 50+ person doing an everyday move ("So my knees feel good" on stairs) selling a **one-time, no-monthly-fee** program with a money-back promise - this ad has run since **Nov 30, 2024** (almost 2 years) [verified]; (b) GOWOD's "What's your mobility score? Take the free test" hook - the same idea as Walkito's new Foot Check, but put in the ad itself. [verified]
- **Our direct competitor just switched route.** "Foot Relief Trainer" (DRAWDOWN BRANDS) now has 8 ads started Nov 11 linking to heelpaintrainer.com (web quiz) next to 4 older App Store ads. Their sister app "Balance Trainer: Stop Falls" still runs ~31 App Store ads and added 8 more on Nov 13. [verified]
- **What it means for question 1 [guess, strong]:** the big earners for this exact audience avoid the setup Walkito used (Meta "App installs" goal -> App Store -> free trial inside iOS). They optimize Meta for a **web purchase** that the pixel can see, and they ask for money at the peak of the quiz, before the app is installed. Walkito's 0 trials is consistent with both a weak goal (installs) and a paywall that asks for a 1-year commitment from people who just met the app.

## 4. What Walkito should copy / test (ranked)

Health ad rules apply: no cure/fix/heal/relieve/pain-free claims, no timeframes, no before/after, no AI people posing as real, no fake testimonials, no fake timers.

1. **A web quiz funnel for Meta traffic (heelpaintrainer.com-style), with Meta optimized for web Purchase or Lead.** Age first (50-59 / 60-69 / 70+), 15-25 questions, email, "Your foot plan is ready", web checkout (Stripe), then "download the app and log in". This is what BetterMe, Yoga-Go, WalkFit, Lasta and now our competitor do. Expected effect: Meta gets a conversion event it can see and optimize for, and the user pays before the install drop-off. Effort: high (3-7 days with FunnelFox / paywallpro or a simple Next.js + Stripe page; app needs a login to unlock a web purchase). [verified that winners do it; effect is guess]
2. **Sell a short first period instead of a year.** Test a 4-week plan (for example $9.99-14.99 for the first 4 weeks, then monthly) as the default on the web, and in the app make monthly the default instead of yearly-with-trial. Or test a **one-time "28-day foot plan" with a money-back promise** like Fit & Flexible for Life (the cleanest 2-year runner for women 50+). Expected effect: fewer people scared off by "$59.99/year" on Apple's sheet. Effort: low in App Store Connect, medium on web. [guess, backed by what winners show]
3. **Add everyday-function questions and a reassurance screen to the quiz.** Copy the BetterMe pattern with foot wording: "Can you stand on one foot to put on your shoes?", "How do your first steps out of bed feel?", "How long are you on your feet each day?". After a "it hurts" answer show a calm reassurance: "We'll start with seated moves" (no promise of results). Expected effect: higher quiz-to-paywall intent. Effort: low. [verified pattern]
4. **One real face from ad to quiz to app.** WalkFit's "Alex Dong" and BetterMe's coach pages show the same person in the ad and the funnel. Walkito already has Dr. Carla Shayman, PT. Put her (real, with permission) in the ad, on the quiz expert screen and on the plan screen. Replace the cartoon mascot in ads with a real 50+ person doing a seated foot move at home - like "So my knees feel good" on stairs, but foot-specific ("Before my first steps", at the bed edge). Expected effect: trust for 55+ women; the cartoon ad won clicks but not buyers. Effort: medium (one filming day). [verified pattern; effect guess]
5. **Put the Foot Check in the ad.** GOWOD's headline is "What's your mobility score? Test your mobility free". Walkito's new Foot Check (ankle bend + balance test) is the same idea. Ad: "How steady are your feet? Take the 2-minute Foot Check." Link to the web quiz or the app. Expected effect: a "test" hook beats "download an app" for curious 55+ users, and it hands them a result before the price. Effort: low (creative only). [verified pattern]

Also worth a quick test: **remove the price worry in the ad itself**, like Hinge ("100% employer covered") - Walkito's honest version is "Try Day 1 free, no card needed" if the 1.0.13 "Not now" path makes that true.

Do NOT copy: AI drama videos and AI "coaches" (Yoga-Go), before/after bodies and weight projections (BetterMe paywall), "promo code applied" + 10-minute countdown (BetterMe, WalkFit), "visible results in 4 weeks", "relieve pain in minutes" (Hinge).

## 5. Which convert 55+ women from Facebook best, and why

**Best: the Ukrainian web2app houses - BetterMe (Chair Yoga, Wall Pilates) and Welltech (Yoga-Go, WalkFit).** [reported volume + verified funnels; "best" is my judgment from spend volume, since nobody publishes conversion rates]
Why:
- They are the biggest spenders in this audience by far (BetterMe ~169K, WalkFit ~115K, Yoga-Go ~99K new ads in Sept 2026), and they keep launching, which only makes sense if it pays back. [reported]
- The offer fits the body of a 60-year-old: chair, Tai Chi, walking, "no floor work", 10-20 minutes. [verified]
- The quiz talks to their age group on screen 1 and 2 and mirrors their daily life (shoes, stairs, knees). [verified]
- They take a small, short payment on the web at the emotional peak ("your plan is ready"), before an App Store install can lose them. [verified]
- Meta optimizes for the purchase, not the install. [reported]

**Second: one-time programs from real 50+ creators (Fit & Flexible for Life).** No subscription fear, money-back promise, real peer. Smaller scale but very long-lived. [verified]

**Weaker for this audience: App-Store-install campaigns with a hard paywall** (LazyFit, Balance Trainer, Foot Relief Trainer's first ads, Walkito). They can buy cheap installs, but the competitor itself moved its heel-pain ads to a web quiz within two weeks. [verified move; reason is guess]

### The 5 things Walkito should copy
1. Web quiz funnel + web checkout + Meta Purchase goal (age first). 
2. A short, cheap first period (4-week plan) or a one-time 28-day plan with money-back, instead of a year.
3. Everyday-life foot questions + a calm reassurance screen after a pain answer.
4. One real PT face from ad to quiz to app; real 50+ person in ads instead of a cartoon.
5. "Take the 2-minute Foot Check" as the ad hook.
