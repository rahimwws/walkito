## 0. Rules for all 5 (read before shooting)

**Offer line.** Stated plainly, the same everywhere, never as the hook:
- On the image or end card (no word "free"): **"$59.99/year · 7-day trial · Cancel anytime in iPhone Settings"**
- In primary text (the one place "free" can appear, inside the trial line only): **"It's a subscription app: 7 days free, then $59.99 a year. Cancel anytime in your iPhone settings."**
- If the paywall changes (monthly $14.99 added, new price), change every ad and the custom product page the same day.

**Never** (from CHECKER NEVER list and B3):
- No cure, fix, heal, relieve, "pain-free", "results", "less pain", or any timeframe ("in 2 weeks", "after 3 weeks"). "5 minutes a day" is fine (session length).
- No "free videos", "free exercises", "start for free", "free" as a headline or on the creative.
- No "you have plantar fasciitis", "your heel pain", "heel pain??" stickers. Use "if...", "anyone who...", "for people who stand all day".
- No AI people, no cartoon mascot, no made-up reviewer, no first-person story from someone who did not live it, no "doctor/PT approved".
- No study numbers like "94% had less pain" or "90% get better" (implied outcome claims for our app). If research is mentioned, keep it generic and in the primary text only: "built on foot strengthening and stretching exercises used in research".
- No pain map with a named condition on screen. No countdowns, no "limited", no "one-time".
- Moves shown must be real Walkito moves, filmed against the real app timer. The moves named below (toe spreads, towel scrunch, heel raises, seated arch stretch) are placeholders: Rahim swaps in the matching moves from the app's Day 1 plan.

**Who appears.** Option A (preferred): one real woman 45-65 that Rahim knows or pays ($50-100), signed model release that says "footage used in paid ads for Walkito, a foot exercise app", face optional, no first-person claims, no lines about her own pain. Option B: feet-only Pexels stock (free for commercial use), terms listed per ad. Note: Rahman called feet-only stock "it sucks" on Nov 3 and chose AI instead; AI people are now ruled out, so Option A is the real answer and Option B is only the fallback.

**Specs.** 4:5 = 1080x1350 (Feed), 1:1 = 1080x1080 (Feed backup), 9:16 = 1080x1920 (Stories/Reels). For 9:16 keep all text between 250 px from the top and 400 px from the bottom (Meta UI covers the rest). Text at least 64 px tall at 1080 wide, bold sans (e.g. Inter Bold / SF Pro Bold), dark on light or white on dark, burned-in captions on every video (most watch muted). Soft music only, no voice needed.

**Common fields.** CTA button: **Install now**. Description line: "Subscription app. Cancel anytime in iPhone Settings." Destination: custom product page, its screenshots and promo text checked for "free videos" lines.

### 1c. What to borrow

1. **The "one move, done right" tutorial** (3162933857238501, 14 days, survived while the cartoon version died). "Save this video" + one move + count + "it's part of a 5-minute routine" + real timer. Ad 2 uses this structure with real feet.
2. **Show the real app screen early and often.** Every long-runner shows the real exercise timer and the plan/quiz screen. Our Ad 3 and Ad 5 do this, with a real phone in a real hand.
3. **Feet/legs + phone timer overlay** (Balance 28 s frame): legs doing the move, timer in a corner. Cheapest high-value shot. Used in Ads 2 and 5.
4. **Myth/explainer hook as a static line** ("Insoles support. Exercises train." is our version of their "what most people get wrong"). Ad 4.
5. **One shared primary text style is fine,** but we keep 2 versions per ad so Meta has something to rotate.
6. **Length:** their winners are 33-51 s. Ours are 15-25 s because our viewers are 45-65 on Facebook Feed and we have no budget to teach a long story. If Ad 2 wins, a 35-40 s cut is the first follow-up.

### 1d. What not to copy

- "heel pain??" sticker, "the sore foot", "for heel pain" (personal-attribute / condition targeting risk).
- "94% had less pain", "90% get better within 10 months", "Less pain" icon, headline "Relieve Plantar Fasciitis From Home" (outcome claims).
- The talking head's first-person story, "After 1 week / 3 weeks / Week 6" progress chips, and the "Teresa B." review card (implied results + likely scripted testimonial, FTC risk).
- "Start today for free" (free framing as the CTA).
- AI-looking people (runner static, balance grandmother).
- Their science graphics: they own that look and the same studies; copying it makes us look like their clone.
- Their move to a web funnel (heelpaintrainer.com): not for us now (B5: web checkout = No).

Our gap (unchanged since Nov 1): nobody shows **real feet doing the real move in a real home with the real app timer**, and nobody shows the daily check-in that changes the plan.
