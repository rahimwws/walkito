Supporting data (not a single case):
- Adapty Meta benchmarks, Q3 2026 (5K apps): optimizing for **Start Trial** costs $20.28 per trial and $57.32 per payer. Optimizing for **Subscribe** costs $42.77 per payer, with D7 ROAS of 75% vs 45%. https://adapty.io/meta-ads-benchmarks - STRONG (large dataset, vendor).
- Airbridge (Nov 2026): install campaigns "get exactly what they ask for". Meta needs about 50 events a week per ad set. At $50/day that is roughly 50 trials a week only if a trial costs about $7. https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/optimize-for-trial-starts-not-installs - MEDIUM (illustrative numbers).
- Airbridge / Phiture: at the median, **94% of installs never start a trial** (install -> trial median 6.2%). https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/why-subscription-app-get-installs-no-paid-subscribers ; https://phiture.com/mobilegrowthstack/integrated-growth-monetization - STRONG.
- Meta health category limits (Jan-Feb 2025): data sources tagged "Health and wellness" (medical conditions, condition-specific apps) can lose mid- and lower-funnel standard events. Install and custom events often stay available. https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-restrictions-health-wellness-campaigns-453094 ; https://martech.health/articles/meta%E2%80%99s-policy-change-what-healthcare-advertisers-need-to-know - STRONG on the rule, unknown for Walkito.

**What caused it, in the community's view:** (1) install optimization buys cheap curious tappers, (2) creative and placement pull younger or wrong-intent people, (3) attribution gaps hide what Meta did deliver. **What fixed it:** a deeper optimization event (purchase or a cleaned trial), creative made for the buyer and served on Facebook placements, and for older audiences, often a different channel (App Store search, web funnel).

## Q4. What actually worked, with before -> after numbers (shortlist)

1. **Purchase optimization on Meta instead of installs:** install -> purchase ~6% -> 18-20% (Bickster, health utility). Adapty Q3 2026: Subscribe optimization means $42.77 vs $57.32 per payer. MEDIUM/STRONG.
2. **Creative and placement aimed at the older buyer, on Facebook:** cost per trial -29%, trial -> paid +2% (Vargas, men 45+). MEDIUM.
3. **Free trial vs discount for a health app:** +140% revenue (Pregnancy+). Adding a trial: +131% paywall conversion (Stardust). MEDIUM.
4. **Trial timeline / reminder:** +27% revenue (Pregnancy+), +13.6% trials (Stardust), reminder screen 14.8% vs 8.7% (Paywall Index). It failed at Flo and Loóna, though. MEDIUM-STRONG.
5. **Lower annual price:** $44.99 beat $59.99 on ARPU, +10% (SellRaze). The health utility settled on $39.99/yr + $59.99 lifetime at ~6% install -> buy. MEDIUM.
6. **Rescuing sheet cancels:** an abandon paywall converts 7.4% (Paywall Index). Failed transactions: 9.2% (Omni). MEDIUM.
7. **$1.99 first month next to the trial:** 19% -> 25% conversion (App Masters community). WEAK-MEDIUM.

## Compliance flags (things in these sources Walkito must NOT copy)

- **Trial on/off toggles:** RevenueCat notes Apple has rejected free-trial toggle paywalls since Feb 2026. Adapty reports toggles that hide auto-renew terms break Guideline 3.1.2(c). https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-redesigns-case-studies ; https://adapty.io/blog/app-to-web-paywalls-ios-one-year-data/
- **"Gray hat" tactics on GTM Sauce:** saying "free" 5-7 times before showing a price (Claim), hiding all prices until a tap (Quitter), a spin wheel that "lands" on 80% off (Cal AI), and Temu/Shein-style "limited spots". These risk Apple 3.1.2 (clear price and terms) and FTC rules on fake urgency and negative-option marketing (ROSCA). Avoid all of them.
- **Hard paywall with no exit:** it boosted trials in two podcast cases, but BoldVoice's refunds rose, and one app was forced by Apple to add a close button. Keep a visible exit.
- **Health claims in ads:** Meta's health policy bans timeframe and result claims. Keep ads free of "fix/cure/pain-free in X days". Use a real person, or label dramatizations. https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939
- **Meta health data:** do not send pain answers or condition details as event parameters (see R6 on BetterHelp and GoodRx). Use neutral event names such as `plans_viewed`, not `heel_pain_user`.

## TOP 5 lessons for Walkito

1. **A 45-65+ fitness app at ~$40/yr on Meta can get zero buyers even with good CTR, and this exact case is on record.** Steve P. Young's 65+ fitness app spent $3-5K at $150/day and got no Meta buyers. Its buyers came from App Store search. Our ~$164 and 0 trials is not unusual for this audience on install campaigns. Why: older buyers need either a search intent moment or a much deeper Meta optimization event. Evidence: MEDIUM.
2. **Install optimization is the main problem, and the fix that worked in health was purchase or subscribe optimization, not more install spend.** Bickster: install -> buy 6% -> 18-20%. Adapty: 25% cheaper payers on Subscribe vs Start Trial. Why: Walkito can't optimize for Start Trial yet. If the health restriction blocks standard events, a custom event like `plans_viewed` (neutral name) is the fallback. Evidence: MEDIUM-STRONG.
3. **Our paywall screen 1 loses 57%, against a normal ~25%, and 0 of 3 people finished Apple's sheet, against a normal ~71%.** Both are far outside normal ranges, so this is a defect, not taste. Why: fix the defect before judging Meta or price. Evidence: STRONG (Paywall Index 3,313 paywalls, RevenueCat 1,170 taps).
4. **For health apps, the trial on annual plus a clear timeline and reminder usually wins. The price that wins clean tests is $39.99-$49.99, not $59.99.** Stardust +131% with a trial at $24.99/yr. Pregnancy+ trial beat a discount by 140%. SellRaze $44.99 beat $59.99. Bick's health utility settled on $39.99/yr. Why: older, price-sensitive Meta traffic sees $59.99 first. Evidence: MEDIUM (several vendor cases, no sample sizes).
5. **Placement and age matter more than the audience settings.** Facebook Feed and Facebook Reels bring older buyers. Instagram Reels brings cheap young trialists. Trial -> paid for 65+ is roughly 4-8x that of 18-24 (Burke). Vargas cut cost per trial 29% by moving spend to Facebook. Why: V2_mascot at 95% of spend is exactly the "Meta picked one cheap-engagement ad" pattern. Evidence: MEDIUM.

## 3 things to test first

1. **Fix and instrument the money path (0 cost, 1-2 days).** Make sure a sandbox and a real new Apple ID both see "7 days free, then $59.99/yr" on Apple's sheet. Log `purchase_cancelled` vs `purchase_failed` with the error code. Add a transaction-abandon screen that offers the same yearly with a longer free trial (14 days) or a one-time lower first year. It must be honest, have a real end, and be shown once. Target: sheet completion above 50% of taps.
2. **Rework paywall screen 1 using the "promise -> reminder -> offer" pattern, with a visible exit on every screen.** Screen 1: "Try Walkito free for 7 days. No payment due now." No buy button and no price on screen 1 (per the Paywall Index data). A small "Not now" is allowed. Screen 2: reminder. Screen 3: yearly with a timeline and real App Store reviews. A/B it against a Stompers-style single page with yearly $59.99 pre-selected and monthly behind "all plans". Target: screen 1 exit under 30%, plans reached above 70%.
3. **Change what Meta optimizes for, and where it serves.** In Events Manager, check whether Walkito's dataset is health-restricted. If Subscribe or Purchase can be optimized, run a small campaign on it. If not, send a neutral custom event (for example `plans_viewed`, or "trial still active after 12 h") and optimize on that. Run 3 or more static and real-person ads made for Facebook Feed and Reels (women 45-65, on-your-feet-all-day hook, no health claims). Cap V2_mascot. In parallel, run Apple Ads exact-match at $20/day as the clean read on paywall quality.

## Where these lessons conflict with Wave 1A (R1-R6)

- **R1 and R3 say show the price and "No payment today" on screen 1, or let people skip to plans.** The Paywall Index finds that top paywalls put no buy button or price on screen 1 (a buy button there: 8.8% vs 13.6%). But Stompers, the one walking app, won with a single page that shows price at once. So: an exit on screen 1 is supported. Putting the price and buy button on screen 1 is contested. Test it, don't assume.
- **R3 says let users do one free session before the trial (Bend model).** Community cases push the other way for hard-paywall apps. Zumba moved the paywall earlier and got more trials. Rootd's early paywall: ~5x revenue. App Masters: "moving the trial later cut trial starts too much". 80-90% of trials start on Day 0. Pregnancy+ day-5 offer (+17%) is a second offer, not a replacement. So: keep the onboarding paywall. A free session can be the path behind "Not now", not a delay before the first ask.
- **R5 says keep the trial on annual and keep the hard paywall.** Community data partly disagrees. Vargas (no trial + purchase optimization, LTV doubled) and Natal and Zumba (no trial on monthly) show no-trial plans can win. Removing the close button doubled trials in one case, while BoldVoice's refunds rose. So: R5 holds for annual. Monthly with no trial is supported. Fully no-trial is only proven where trust already exists.
- **R5 says do not lead with weekly.** Most community data agrees for health. But Steve P. Young's 3x from a weekly trial and Rash ID's +50% trial -> paid after adding a weekly anchor suggest a weekly plan as a visible anchor (not the default) is worth a test.
- **R2 says move to Start Trial optimization when eligible.** Adapty's Q3 2026 data and Bickster say go straight to Subscribe or Purchase if you can. Start Trial optimization buys trials but costs ~34% more per payer. Several experts also warn Meta drifts to young cheap trialists when optimizing for trials.
- **R4 and R6 favor Apple Ads as the clean channel.** The 65+ fitness case strongly supports this (buyers only came from search). But Bickster found "Meta converted even better than Apple Ads" once they optimized for purchases. Both can be true. Apple Ads is the faster diagnostic, and Meta can still work with the right event.
- **R6 says not to add web checkout inside the app.** That is consistent with Zumba (-25% to -35% initial conversion). But Built With Science, Blinkist and Rise say ad-to-web quiz funnels are how they reach 40-60-year-olds at scale on Meta. This matches R6's "ad-to-web works, in-app web button doesn't".
